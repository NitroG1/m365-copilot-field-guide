Six stations, about 55 minutes, two sittings. By the end, Cowork on your Mac turns a meeting transcript into grounded action items and a draft email, first by hand, then as a reusable skill, then automatically when a transcript lands in your inbox. That is the agent you described, built on the Mac so it transfers to work.

Files you need, already in your Downloads folder on the Mac (links here for any other device): sample-transcript.md (a made-up 26-minute meeting) and meeting-actions-SKILL.md (the skill file, written for you).

The mental model for Cowork, since you asked

Chat is you doing the work with an adviser beside you. Cowork is you delegating the work to a junior colleague who has access to your files, mail and calendar. You give the goal, it writes a plan, it does the steps across apps, and it stops to ask before anything that sends, posts, creates or changes something. The side panel is its desk: progress, the files it was given, the files it made, the skills it loaded, the permissions you granted.

Sitting 1, about 30 minutes: specific prompts, grounded answers, the agent by hand

1. Specific prompts: the five-part frame

10 min. Microsoft Word, the document from Session 1.

Goal: feel the difference a specific prompt makes, and fix the Word problem from Session 1 where "half the length" rewrote the whole document.

You said my prompts need to be more specific. You are right, and there is a frame for it. Every prompt to Copilot carries five parts: Scope (what exactly to touch), Goal (what good looks like), Source (what to use and what not to), Format (the shape of the answer), Check (what to tell you if it cannot comply). Vague prompts leave Copilot to guess the scope, and it guesses big.

  1. Open How I will use Copilot this quarter in Word. Select one paragraph only.
  2. Use the Copilot icon beside the selection and send prompt A, the vague one. Watch what it changes. Undo (⌘Z).
  3. Select the same paragraph. Send prompt B, the specific one. Watch what it changes.
  4. In the Copilot pane, send prompt C. It asks Copilot to rewrite your own vague prompt into the frame. Keep what it gives you.
Prompt A (vague, on purpose)Make this half the length.
Prompt B (specific)Rewrite only the selected paragraph. Leave every other paragraph unchanged. Cut it to half its current word count, keep the same meaning and the same first sentence, no bullet points. If you cannot do it without changing the meaning, tell me instead of rewriting.
Prompt C (Copilot pane)I want to get better at prompting you. Take this prompt: "Summarise this document and tell me what my manager will ask." Rewrite it using five parts: Scope, Goal, Source, Format, Check. Show the rewritten prompt only, then run it.
What a power user watches for
  • Did prompt A touch more than the selection again? Did prompt B stay inside it? That one difference is the whole lesson.
  • Prompt C turns Copilot into your prompt editor. Use it whenever an answer disappoints: ask it to rewrite your prompt before you rewrite your prompt.
  • The Check part is what stops silent failure. Without it, Copilot does something rather than nothing.

2. The grounding test

10 min. Copilot app, Chat tab, new chat. Needs sample-transcript.md.

Goal: prove to yourself whether Copilot will stay inside a source, and learn the test that catches it when it does not. This is the answer to your Researcher concern, in miniature.

  1. New chat. Attach sample-transcript.md from Downloads.
  2. Send prompt A. Check every evidence quote against the transcript. There are eight or nine genuine action items in it, depending on how you count one of them; the answer key is under "what to watch for".
  3. Send prompt B, the trap. The transcript does not contain that information.
  4. Send prompt C.
Prompt AUsing only the attached transcript, list every action item: something a named person agreed to do. For each one give the action, the owner, the due date exactly as stated, and a quote under 20 words with its timestamp as evidence. If an owner or date is not stated, write "not stated". Do not include tentative items like "maybe" or "park it". Table format.
Prompt B (the trap)How many pilot customers cancelled their subscription, and what reasons did they give?
Prompt CList everything in the transcript that you deliberately left out of the action items, and say in one line each why it did not qualify.
What a power user watches for, with the answer key
  • The genuine action items: Marcus confirms the data clause in writing today; Priya returns the contract draft Thursday; Dana calls the two inactive customers this week; Dana has call notes by Monday the 20th; Marcus reports on Friday whether the export error is a timeout; Dana mentions the export issue on the call with no dates; Ryan chases finance for pricing; Priya finishes the training deck the week of the 27th; Ryan sets up a dry run on the 30th. That is nine lines because Dana's call and her notes can be counted as one or two. Either is fine.
  • Not action items: the status page (parked), Marcus wanting "a week to fix it" (Ryan said not to commit). If either shows up as an action, that is inference, and that is the failure your agent must not make.
  • Prompt B: the only honest answer is that the transcript does not say. Anything else is invented.
  • Prompt C is the quality check you can run on any extraction. What it left out tells you more than what it kept.

3. The agent by hand, in Cowork

10 min. Copilot app, Cowork tab. Needs sample-transcript.md.

Goal: run your whole agent once as a single Cowork task: transcript in, grounded action items, draft email with the transcript attached, approval, send to yourself.

  1. Click the Cowork tab. Drag sample-transcript.md onto the chat box.
  2. Paste the prompt. Swap in your own email address.
  3. Watch the side panel on the right: Progress, Input folder, Skills. Note which built-in skills it loads (expect Email, maybe Word).
  4. When the approval card appears, read the draft. Check the attachment is there. Then approve with Send.
  5. Check your inbox.
PromptRead the attached meeting transcript. Using only what the transcript says, extract: (1) action items as a table with columns #, Action, Owner, Due, Evidence quote with timestamp; (2) decisions, quoted; (3) open questions and parked items. Rules: never infer an owner or date, write "not stated" instead; tentative items ("maybe", "park it", "not committing yet") are not actions. Then draft an email to [your email address] with subject "Action items: Q4 launch readiness sync (2026-10-14)", the table and lists in the body, the sentence "Reply if anything here is wrong; this was prepared from the transcript, not from memory", and the transcript file attached. Show me the draft and ask before sending.
What a power user watches for
  • Did it show a plan before acting? Did it ask before sending, as told? The approval card should show the full email.
  • Compare its table with station 2. Same eight items? Same "not stated" where the transcript is silent?
  • The email in your inbox is the first output of your agent. Everything after this is making it repeatable.
Sitting 2, about 25 minutes: make it a skill, make it automatic, plan the move to work

4. Install the Meeting Actions skill

10 min. Copilot app, Cowork tab. Needs meeting-actions-SKILL.md.

Goal: turn the prompt from station 3 into a skill Cowork loads by itself whenever you hand it a transcript. I wrote the skill file; you install it and read Cowork's quality report on it.

The skill file is the same idea as a SKILL.md in Claude Code: a name, a description that says when to fire, then instructions. Open it in any text editor first and read it. You should be able to change any line of it later.
  1. In Cowork, find Customize in the left navigation. Open the Skills tab.
  2. Click the small arrow next to Add, choose Upload skill, and pick meeting-actions-SKILL.md from Downloads.
  3. Cowork evaluates the skill and shows a Skill Report with a score out of 100 across four parts: trigger clarity, instruction specificity, scope boundaries, robustness. Write the score and any "needs work" line into the feedback box.
  4. If Customize or Upload is not there on your personal account, use the fallback: in OneDrive create the folder Documents/Cowork/skills/meeting-actions/, copy the file in, and rename it SKILL.md. Cowork finds it at the start of the next session.
  5. Start a new Cowork session, attach sample-transcript.md, and send the prompt. It is deliberately short: the skill should carry the rest.
Prompt (short on purpose)Action items from this meeting, please. Draft the email to me but do not send it.
What a power user watches for
  • Did "Meeting Actions" appear as a chip in the Skills section of the side panel? That means the description triggered it. If it did not, the fix is the description line in the file, not the prompt.
  • Did the short prompt produce the same quality as the long one in station 3? That is the point of a skill: the instructions live in the file, not in your head.
  • The Skill Report is Microsoft grading your agent. Keep the score; we will try to raise it next session.

5. Make it automatic with an email trigger

10 min. Copilot app, Cowork tab, plus your mail app.

Goal: the agent runs on its own when a transcript arrives, and you only approve the result.

  1. In Cowork, new session. Paste the prompt.
  2. Cowork should answer with a Set up trigger? card showing When, Run in, What it does, and the permissions it needs. Read it, then click Set up.
  3. Now fire it: from your mail app, send yourself an email with the subject Transcript: Q4 launch readiness sync and sample-transcript.md attached.
  4. Open Automations in the left navigation (or My tasks, filter Automations). Watch for the run. It may take a few minutes; Microsoft rate-limits triggers.
  5. Open the run, check the draft, approve it. Then pause or delete the trigger so it does not keep firing on test mail.
PromptWhenever I receive an email whose subject starts with "Transcript:", use the Meeting Actions skill on the attached transcript and draft the follow-up email to me for my review. Never send anything without my approval.
What a power user watches for
  • Does the trigger card name the skill? If it only shows your words, the skill may still load at run time. Check the run's side panel.
  • How long between the email landing and the run appearing? Write it down; at work the recap email arrives minutes after a meeting ends, so this delay is your agent's real latency.
  • If personal Cowork has no triggers yet (it is in preview), note it and fall back to a scheduled prompt: "Every weekday at 5 pm, check my inbox for emails from today with a subject starting Transcript: and run the Meeting Actions skill on each."

6. Moving it to work, and your verdict

5 min. No app needed.

What changes when this agent moves to the work account, so nothing you learned here is wasted.

  1. The transcript source changes. At work Cowork can read Teams meetings directly through "Add work context", and the Meetings skill prepares meeting intelligence. The trigger becomes the recap email Teams sends after a recorded meeting, which starts with the meeting name. Everything else in the skill file stays the same.
  2. The skill can be shared. On a work account the skill's detail page has a Share button for specific people in the organisation. That is Stage 3 of your mission, being the team's Copilot person, already in reach.
  3. Approvals matter more. The email goes to real attendees. Keep "ask before sending" in the skill. Never switch on "always allow" for sending.
  4. What to carry over by hand: the skill file (copy it into the work OneDrive at the same path), your five-part prompt frame, and the grounding test from station 2.
